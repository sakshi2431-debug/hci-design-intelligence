# Ethics, privacy, and deceptive design

Use this reference for consent, subscriptions, monetization, personalization, data collection, persuasive design, vulnerable users, or high-stakes decisions.

## Source anchors

- `REGULATORY` — [FTC: Bringing Dark Patterns to Light](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light).
- `PROFESSIONAL` — [ACM Code of Ethics](https://www.acm.org/code-of-ethics).
- `RISK FRAMEWORK` — [NIST Privacy Framework](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework).

Regulatory applicability varies by jurisdiction, product, audience, and date. Use these sources to identify risks; obtain qualified legal review for legal conclusions.

## User-rights audit

Inspect whether the interface:

- states material terms before commitment;
- makes accepting and refusing genuinely understandable and comparably actionable;
- avoids preselection, obstruction, repeated pressure, disguised advertising, false urgency, hidden costs, and confirmshaming;
- makes cancellation, deletion, export, correction, and withdrawal reasonably findable and proportionate to signup;
- collects only data needed for an explained purpose;
- separates required processing from optional personalization or marketing;
- prevents accidental purchases, subscriptions, sharing, or destructive actions;
- protects children and people in vulnerable or high-pressure contexts;
- preserves an accessible nonvisual and keyboard path to every privacy choice;
- avoids using experimentation to optimize manipulation or concealment.

## Consent test

Valid-looking interface mechanics are insufficient. Ask whether the user can understand what will happen, who receives data, why it is needed, how long it persists, whether refusal changes service, and how to reverse the choice. Test comprehension and ability to act, not merely checkbox completion.

## Persuasion boundary

Persuasion supports an informed user goal. Manipulation exploits attention, emotion, information asymmetry, friction, or defaults to advance another party's goal against the user's interests. Evaluate both the local screen and the entire journey; deceptive asymmetry often appears across signup, renewal, cancellation, and recovery.

## Reporting

Label the evidence source and distinguish:

- observed interface behavior;
- plausible ethical or privacy harm;
- applicable published guidance;
- confirmed legal requirement;
- recommendation and validation plan.

Never call a pattern illegal without current jurisdiction-specific authority.

# Coursework synthesis: Interaction Design Project Practice

Public-safe original synthesis derived from user-supplied graduate coursework. It does not reproduce readings, assignments, rubrics, private examples, or participant data. Treat it as a course-informed lens, not as independent empirical authority.

## Use this reference when

Scoping an interaction-design project, collaborating with stakeholders, planning research, coordinating teams, or producing decision-ready deliverables.

## Working model

- A project is a sequence of decisions and relationships, not just a sequence of deliverables.
- Establish the problem, people, stakeholders, constraints, risks, success criteria, decision rights, and evidence needs before committing to a solution.
- Map stakeholders by influence, impact, knowledge, responsibility, and dependency; include affected people who lack formal power.
- Treat kickoff and follow-up as expectation-design: clarify scope, roles, communication, access, approvals, risks, and next decisions.
- Plan research and synthesis around what the team must learn, not around a fixed list of methods.
- Make handoffs usable: show rationale, evidence, states, dependencies, unresolved questions, and verification criteria.

## Decision prompts

1. What is the current decision and who has authority over it?
2. Which people are affected, represented, absent, or able to block progress?
3. What is in scope, explicitly out of scope, and still uncertain?
4. What evidence will change the direction?
5. Which dependencies, access needs, and organizational constraints could invalidate the plan?
6. What must the next collaborator understand or do with the deliverable?

## Guardrails

- Do not confuse stakeholder agreement with user evidence.
- Do not overpromise outcomes before access and constraints are known.
- Preserve disagreement, assumptions, and unresolved risk in the project record.

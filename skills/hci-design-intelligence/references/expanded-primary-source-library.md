# Expanded primary-source library

Use this library when the compact foundation and open-access theory guides do not provide enough depth. It extends the skill into collaborative work, design exploration, cognitive accessibility, service and systems design, trustworthy AI, appropriate reliance, and rigorous measurement.

This is a routing bibliography, not a substitute for reading the source. Links and access routes were checked on 2026-11-23. A link does not grant permission to redistribute a source. Summarize in original words, preserve attribution, and verify the version, license, population, task, and boundary conditions before making a consequential claim.

## Access labels

- **PRIMARY—OPEN:** original work is openly readable from its publisher, author, institution, standards body, or recognized repository.
- **AUTHOR MANUSCRIPT:** lawful author- or institution-hosted copy of a published work.
- **OFFICIAL GUIDANCE:** first-party standard, government framework, or organizational guidance.
- **ACM note:** the ACM Digital Library has been free to read since 1 January 2026, so ACM papers are labeled PRIMARY—OPEN (relabeled 2026-11-23). Open to read is not a reuse license.
- **CANONICAL CITATION:** stable record for a foundational source; full text may require library access.

## How to use this library

1. Choose the decision first; do not cite sources merely because they are famous.
2. Read the original or lawful manuscript for claims that affect safety, rights, research design, or system authority.
3. Record the construct, population, task, apparatus, measures, and important limitations.
4. State whether the source is normative, empirical, theoretical, applied guidance, or synthesis.
5. Translate the source into a testable project hypothesis or requirement.
6. Pair theory with direct evidence from the people, work, system, and environment in scope.
7. When sources conflict, preserve the conflict and identify the evidence needed to resolve it.

# Coursework synthesis: Digital Accessibility

Public-safe original synthesis derived from user-supplied graduate coursework. It does not reproduce readings, assignments, rubrics, private examples, or participant data. Treat it as a course-informed lens, not as independent empirical authority.

## Use this reference when

Framing accessibility needs, evaluating flows, integrating accessibility into product work, or distinguishing compliance from usable inclusion.

## Working model

- Disability emerges through interaction among people, environments, technologies, policies, and tasks; avoid reducing it to a checklist or medical label.
- Design for functional needs across vision, hearing, speech, mobility, cognition, language, attention, memory, and combinations of these.
- Standards provide testable requirements, but conformance does not by itself establish usability, inclusion, dignity, or task success.
- Keyboard, nonvisual, magnification, voice, switch, and cognitive interaction must be considered across complete flows and states.
- Accessibility belongs in research, requirements, content, design, implementation, procurement, quality assurance, and governance.

## Decision prompts

1. Who could be excluded from this task, under which conditions?
2. What information, operation, timing, movement, sensory, or cognitive demands exist?
3. Can all controls be perceived, understood, reached, operated, and recovered from?
4. Are semantics, names, roles, values, focus, reading order, status, and errors available programmatically?
5. Does the experience remain usable with zoom, reflow, text spacing, keyboard-only use, and assistive technology?
6. Which claims require code inspection, manual testing, or disabled-participant research?

## Guardrails

- Never infer screen-reader behavior, keyboard behavior, or conformance from a screenshot alone.
- Label automated results as signals, not proof.
- Separate confirmed defects, plausible risks, inclusive-design opportunities, and untested requirements.
- Preserve equivalent purpose and control; an alternate route should not be second-class.

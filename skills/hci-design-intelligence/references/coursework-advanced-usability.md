# Coursework synthesis: Advanced Usability

Public-safe original synthesis derived from user-supplied graduate coursework. It does not reproduce readings, assignments, rubrics, private examples, or participant data. Treat it as a course-informed lens, not as independent empirical authority.

## Use this reference when

Planning or critiquing usability evaluation, combining evidence, moderating studies, choosing metrics, or communicating findings to stakeholders.

## Working model

- Evaluation exists to change a decision, not merely to produce findings.
- Start with the product decision, risk, users, context, and evidence threshold; then select the method.
- Distinguish formative work that improves a design from summative work that estimates performance against defined criteria.
- A credible study aligns recruitment, tasks, environment, measures, analysis, and reporting with the target use context.
- Moderation is an interaction-design problem: prompts, timing, neutrality, rapport, and follow-ups affect the evidence produced.
- Combine behavioral evidence, attitudinal evidence, product analytics, operational evidence, and expert inspection without pretending they answer the same question.

## Decision prompts

1. What decision will this evaluation change?
2. Which users, tasks, contexts, and failure consequences must be represented?
3. What would count as disconfirming evidence?
4. Which measures describe task performance, perception, experience quality, or business outcome?
5. Could instrumentation, sampling, facilitation, or interpretation create the apparent result?
6. What can the evidence establish, and what remains an inference?

## Method cautions

- Eye tracking can reveal visual attention patterns, not comprehension, intention, or causality by itself.
- Product analytics can reveal patterns at scale, not why a pattern occurred without further evidence.
- Experiments estimate effects within their design and population; statistical significance is not product importance.
- A score is useful only when its construct, administration, comparison point, and decision rule are clear.
- Stakeholder storytelling should preserve uncertainty, counterevidence, and the chain from evidence to recommendation.

## Output pattern

State: decision, method fit, observed evidence, limitations, interpretation, recommendation, confidence, and next discriminating test.

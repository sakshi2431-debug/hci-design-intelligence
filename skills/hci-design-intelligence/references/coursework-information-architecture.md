# Coursework synthesis: Information Architecture

Public-safe original synthesis derived from user-supplied graduate coursework. It does not reproduce readings, assignments, rubrics, private examples, or participant data. Treat it as a course-informed lens, not as independent empirical authority.

## Use this reference when

Structuring information, navigation, labeling, search, cross-channel journeys, or the conceptual organization of a product or service.

## Working model

- Information architecture connects people, content, context, and systems so that information can be found, understood, navigated, and acted on.
- Begin with research into users' language, tasks, expectations, content, organizational constraints, and ecosystem—not a sitemap.
- Treat organization, labeling, navigation, search, metadata, and controlled vocabularies as interdependent.
- Represent the architecture in forms suited to the decision: inventories, models, taxonomies, flows, sitemaps, navigation schemas, content models, and prototypes.
- Evaluate both local findability and the larger conceptual model across channels and time.

## Decision prompts

1. What do people need to understand or accomplish, and what language do they use?
2. What content exists, who owns it, how does it change, and what must be removed?
3. Which organization schemes match the domain and users' mental models?
4. How do navigation, search, labels, and metadata work together?
5. Where do people enter, switch channels, lose context, or encounter inconsistent structures?
6. Could the architecture manipulate attention, hide costs or choices, or privilege institutional needs over human goals?

## Validation

Use methods appropriate to the uncertainty: content audit, stakeholder and user research, card sorting, tree testing, search-log analysis, first-click testing, prototype tasks, and cross-channel journey evaluation. Do not treat one method as sufficient proof.

## Output pattern

State the user and content model, structural proposal, label rationale, navigation and search behavior, edge cases, governance needs, evidence, and validation plan.

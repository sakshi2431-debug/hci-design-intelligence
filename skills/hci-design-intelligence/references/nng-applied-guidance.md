# Nielsen Norman Group applied guidance

Use NN/g as applied expert guidance, not as a standards body or substitute for project research. Link to the relevant article when making a material claim.

## Ten usability heuristics

Use the [official overview](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/):

1. **Visibility of system status:** keep people informed with timely, meaningful feedback.
2. **Match between system and the real world:** use people's language, concepts, and real-world ordering.
3. **User control and freedom:** provide clear exits, undo, and reversibility.
4. **Consistency and standards:** follow internal consistency and platform conventions.
5. **Error prevention:** remove error-prone conditions or confirm before risky actions.
6. **Recognition rather than recall:** keep options and context visible instead of relying on memory.
7. **Flexibility and efficiency of use:** offer accelerators and customization for frequent users.
8. **Aesthetic and minimalist design:** remove information that competes with what matters.
9. **Help users recognize, diagnose, and recover from errors:** use plain-language errors that point to a solution.
10. **Help and documentation:** provide findable, task-focused help when the interface alone is not enough.

Lead each finding with the official heuristic name; the descriptions above are paraphrases.

Heuristics often overlap; identify the mechanism rather than inflating issue counts. Phrase findings as “This design appears to violate…” until user evidence confirms impact.

## Heuristic evaluation

1. Define target users, tasks, platforms, and scope.
2. Use several independent evaluators when possible.
3. Inspect complete flows and states, not isolated screens.
4. Consolidate duplicates after independent review.
5. Rate severity using frequency/reach, impact, persistence/recovery, and task criticality.
6. Separate heuristic violations from bugs, content defects, accessibility criteria, and business concerns.
7. Validate severe or uncertain findings with representative users.

## Research-method routing

Use the [NN/g methods landscape](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/which-ux-research-methods/) to distinguish attitudinal from behavioral methods, qualitative from quantitative methods, and context of use. Do not treat the matrix as a rigid taxonomy.

## Information architecture

Use applied guidance for card sorting, tree testing, labels, navigation, search, and information scent. Validate the actual audience vocabulary. A familiar global-navigation pattern does not prove that categories or labels match users' models.

## Writing recommendations

Connect the heuristic to observable design evidence and a user consequence:

`Evidence → heuristic mechanism → affected task → risk → recommendation → validation`

Avoid “NN/g says” as the full rationale.

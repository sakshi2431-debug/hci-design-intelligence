# Coursework synthesis: Service Design

Public-safe original synthesis derived from user-supplied graduate coursework. It does not reproduce readings, assignments, rubrics, private examples, or participant data. Treat it as a course-informed lens, not as independent empirical authority.

## Use this reference when

Designing cross-channel services, mapping frontstage and backstage systems, facilitating co-design, or evaluating service outcomes.

## Working model

- A service is a coordinated system of interactions, people, policies, processes, environments, data, and infrastructure—not an interface alone.
- Map the experience across time and channels, including what customers see, what employees do, backstage processes, support systems, evidence, and failure recovery.
- Use service blueprints to expose dependencies and responsibility, not merely to document an ideal journey.
- Co-design requires choices about participation, power, compensation, accessibility, facilitation, interpretation, and how contributions affect decisions.
- Consider the interaction, service, organizational, ecosystem, and institutional levels; local improvements can move harm elsewhere.

## Decision prompts

1. What outcome is the service meant to enable for people and the organization?
2. Which actors, touchpoints, channels, handoffs, policies, data, and infrastructures shape that outcome?
3. Where do waiting, ambiguity, repeated effort, exclusion, or responsibility gaps occur?
4. What happens when a channel, employee, partner, or automated system fails?
5. Who participates in design, who decides, and how is power handled?
6. What behaviors are being influenced, and is the value exchange transparent and defensible?

## Blueprint minimum

Include user actions, frontstage interactions, backstage actions, support processes, data or systems, rules, ownership, timing, evidence, failure paths, recovery, and measures.

## Guardrails

- Do not optimize the visible touchpoint while ignoring employee burden or inaccessible backstage policy.
- Distinguish a case example from evidence that a pattern will work elsewhere.
- Pair service performance with human outcomes, equity, accessibility, and recovery measures.

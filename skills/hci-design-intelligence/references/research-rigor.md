# Research rigor and ethics

Read this reference when a study supports product, academic, policy, safety, or causal claims. The ordinary method-routing overview remains in [research-and-evaluation.md](research-and-evaluation.md).

## Construct and claim discipline

1. State the decision, intended claim, population, context, and unit of analysis.
2. Define each construct operationally. Do not use “engagement,” “trust,” “ease,” or “success” without specifying the observable or reported measure.
3. Choose a method capable of supporting the claim. Discovery research can reveal mechanisms and unmet needs; it does not estimate prevalence without a suitable sampling design.
4. Record alternative explanations and disconfirming evidence.
5. Distinguish exploratory from confirmatory analysis before examining outcomes.

## Sampling

- Define inclusion, exclusion, recruitment channels, relevant experience, accessibility needs, and context.
- Use purposeful variation for formative qualitative work and probability-based or appropriately modeled samples for population estimates.
- Treat convenience recruitment, customers who volunteer, and platform users as selected populations.
- Justify sample size from information needs, heterogeneity, precision, power, risk, and study design—not a universal UX number.
- Track recruitment failures and who remains unrepresented.

## Measurement quality

- **Validity:** does the measure support the intended interpretation in this context?
- **Reliability:** is measurement sufficiently consistent for the decision?
- **Sensitivity:** can it detect a meaningful difference without ceiling or floor effects?
- **Reactivity:** does observation, think-aloud, instrumentation, or prompting change the behavior?
- **Accessibility:** can participants understand and use the instrument without construct-irrelevant barriers?

Use validated scales according to their scoring instructions and licensing conditions. Do not rewrite scale items and continue to call the result the original instrument.

## Qualitative analysis

Maintain an audit trail from raw evidence to codes, themes, claims, and recommendations. Define the coding approach, preserve negative cases, separate participant language from researcher interpretation, and use multiple analysts when it materially improves credibility. Agreement statistics are not automatically required; if used, explain what was coded, by whom, and why the statistic is meaningful.

Do not turn mention counts into prevalence estimates. Quote narrowly, protect identity, and retain enough context to avoid misrepresentation.

## Experiments and A/B tests

Predefine the hypothesis, assignment unit, population, primary outcome, guardrails, minimum meaningful effect, analysis, exclusions, duration, and stopping rule. Check randomization, sample-ratio mismatch, contamination, interference, novelty, seasonality, instrumentation changes, missingness, and multiple comparisons.

Report effect sizes and uncertainty alongside significance. A statistically detectable change may be practically trivial or harmful on an unmeasured outcome.

## Surveys

Map every item to a decision or construct. Avoid double-barreled, leading, loaded, ambiguous, or unnecessarily hypothetical questions. Use exhaustive and mutually sensible response options, randomization only when it does not damage meaning, and cognitive pretesting before launch. Report invitation, completion, exclusions, breakoff, and weighting.

## Participant rights and data governance

Use understandable consent, voluntary participation, appropriate compensation, accessible materials, minimized data collection, secure handling, retention limits, withdrawal procedures, and additional safeguards for vulnerable populations or sensitive topics. Institutional-review requirements depend on the organization and research purpose; do not declare a project exempt without the responsible authority.

Useful ethical anchors include the [Belmont Report](https://www.hhs.gov/ohrp/regulations-and-policy/belmont-report/) and the [ACM Code of Ethics](https://www.acm.org/code-of-ethics). Apply the governing institutional policy where it is stricter.

## Reporting minimum

Report purpose, dates, product/version, researchers, participant and recruitment details, context, materials, procedure, operational definitions, exclusions, analysis, findings, uncertainty, limitations, conflicts of interest, data handling, and the decision each finding informs. Keep raw observations, interpretation, and recommendations separable.
